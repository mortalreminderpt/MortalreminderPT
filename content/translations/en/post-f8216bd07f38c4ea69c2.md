## Introduction

This chapter introduces three scheduling algorithms suitable for **early batch processing systems**.

|Algorithm|Preemptive|Advantages|Disadvantages|Considers running and waiting time|Causes starvation|
|--|--|--|--|
|FCFS|Non-preemptive|Fair, simple to implement|Disadvantageous to short jobs|Waiting time √<br />Running time ×|No|
|SJF/SPF|Non-preemptive, also has a preemptive version called Shortest Remaining Time Next (SRTN)|Nearly the shortest average waiting time/turnaround time|Disadvantageous to long jobs, may cause starvation; <br />difficult to achieve true shortest job first|Waiting time ×<br />Running time √|Yes|
|HRRN|Non-preemptive|A compromise between FCFS and SJF, considering both waiting and running time|-|Waiting time √<br />Running time √|No|

These algorithms mainly care about fairness to users, average turnaround time, average waiting time, and other metrics that evaluate the overall performance of the system, but they do not care about "response time" and do not distinguish the urgency of tasks.
Therefore, for users, interactivity is poor. Thus, these three algorithms are generally suitable for **early batch processing systems**. Of course, the FCFS algorithm is also often used in combination with other algorithms and still plays a very important role today.
Scheduling algorithms suitable for interactive systems will be introduced in the next chapter.

## FCFS Algorithm

### Characteristics of the FCFS Algorithm

|Algorithm name|First Come First Serve (FCFS)|
|--|--|
|Algorithm idea|Mainly considered from the perspective of "fairness" (similar to the example of queuing to buy things in our daily life)|
|Algorithm rule|Serve according to the **order of arrival** of jobs/processes|
|Used for job/process scheduling|When used for **job scheduling**, it considers which job arrives first in the **backing queue**; <br />when used for **process scheduling**, it considers which process arrives first in the **ready queue**|
|Preemptive or not|Non-preemptive algorithm|
|Advantages and disadvantages|Advantages: fair, simple to implement; <br />Disadvantages: short jobs (processes) queued behind long jobs (processes) need to wait for a long time, and the weighted turnaround time is large, so the user experience is poor for short jobs. That is, **the FCFS algorithm is advantageous to long jobs and disadvantageous to short jobs**|
|Causes starvation|No|

### Example
The arrival times of each process at the ready queue and the required running times are shown in the following table. Using the First Come First Serve scheduling algorithm, calculate the waiting time, average waiting time, turnaround time, average turnaround time, weighted turnaround time, and average weighted turnaround time of each process.

|Process|Arrival time|Running time|
|--|--|--|
|P1|0|7|
|P2|2|4|
|P3|4|1|
|P4|5|4|

Answer:

FCFS schedules according to the **order of arrival**; in fact, the one that has waited the longest is served first.

Therefore, the scheduling order is P1>P2>P3>P4, as shown in the figure.

Turnaround time = Completion time - Arrival time
Weighted turnaround time = Turnaround time / Running time
Waiting time = Turnaround time - Running time

Note: The processes in this example are all purely computational processes. After a process arrives, it is either waiting or running.
If a process also has I/O operations in addition to computation, its waiting time is turnaround time - running time - I/O operation time.

|Process|P1|P2|P3|P4|
|--|--|--|--|--|
|Turnaround time|7-0=7|11-2=9|12-4=8|16-5=11|
|Weighted turnaround time|7/7=1|9/4=2.25|8/1=8|11/4=2.75|
|Waiting time|7-7=0|9-4=5|8-1=7|11-4=7|

Average turnaround time = (7+9+8+11) / 4 = 8.75

Average weighted turnaround time = (1+2.25+8+2.75) / 4 = 3.5

Average waiting time = (0+5+7+7) / 4 = 4.75

## SJF Algorithm

### Characteristics of the SJF Algorithm

|Algorithm name|Shortest Job First (SJF)|
|--|--|
|Algorithm idea|Pursue **the minimum average waiting time, the minimum average turnaround time, and the minimum average weighted turnaround time**|
|Algorithm rule|The shortest job/process is served first (the so-called "shortest" means **the shortest required service time**)|
|Used for job/process scheduling|It can be used for both job scheduling and process scheduling. When used for process scheduling, it is called the Shortest Process First (SPF) algorithm|
|Preemptive or not|SJF/SPF is a non-preemptive algorithm. However, there is also a preemptive version—the **Shortest Remaining Time Next (SRTN)** algorithm|
|Advantages and disadvantages|Advantages: nearly the shortest average waiting time and average turnaround time; <br />Disadvantages: unfair. **Advantageous to short jobs and disadvantageous to long jobs.** It may cause **starvation**. In addition, the running time of a job/process is provided by the user and may not be true, so true shortest job first may not be achieved|
|Causes starvation|Yes. If short jobs/processes keep arriving, long jobs/processes may not be served for a long time, causing "starvation". If they are never served, it is called "starvation to death"|

### Example
The arrival times of each process at the ready queue and the required running times are shown in the following table. Using the non-preemptive Shortest Job First scheduling algorithm, calculate the waiting time, average waiting time, turnaround time, average turnaround time, weighted turnaround time, and average weighted turnaround time of each process.

|Process|Arrival time|Running time|
|--|--|--|
|P1|0|7|
|P2|2|4|
|P3|4|1|
|P4|5|4|

Answer:

The Shortest Job/Process First scheduling algorithm selects **the job/process that has currently arrived and has the shortest running time** at each scheduling decision.

Therefore, the scheduling order is P1>P3>P2>P4

### Preemptive Version—SRTN Algorithm

The arrival times of each process at the ready queue and the required running times are shown in the following table. Using the preemptive Shortest Job First scheduling algorithm, calculate the waiting time, average waiting time, turnaround time, average turnaround time, weighted turnaround time, and average weighted turnaround time of each process.

|Process|Arrival time|Running time|
|--|--|--|
|P1|0|7|
|P2|2|4|
|P3|4|1|
|P4|5|4|

Answer:

The **Shortest Remaining Time Next algorithm** requires **scheduling whenever a process joins the ready queue and changes it**. If the **remaining time** of the newly arrived process is **shorter** than the remaining time of the currently running process, the new process **preempts the processor**, and the currently running process returns to the ready queue.
In addition, **scheduling is also required when a process completes.**

Note that when a new process arrives, the ready queue changes, and the above rules must be used for checking.
Below, Pn=m means that the current remaining time of process Pn is m. The situation at each moment is as follows:

|Moment|Remaining time of different processes|
|--|--|
|0 (P1 arrives)|**P1=7**|
|2 (P2 arrives)|P1=5, **P2=4**|
|4 (P3 arrives)|P1=5, P2=2, **P3=1**|
|5 (P3 completes, P4 arrives)|P1=5, **P2=2**, P4=4|
|7 (P2 completes)|P1=5, **P4=4**|
|11 (P4 completes)|**P1=5**|

|Process|P1|P2|P3|P4|

|Turnaround time|16-0=16|7-2=5|5-4=1|11-5=6|
|Weighted turnaround time|16/7=2.28|5/4=1.25|1/1=1|6/4=1.5|

Average turnaround time = (16+5+1+6) / 4 = 7

Average weighted turnaround time = (2.28+1.25+1+1.5) / 4 = 1.50

Average waiting time = (9+1+0+2) / 4 = 3

Compared with the SJF/SPF algorithm, the preemptive version obviously has lower values for these metrics.

### Note

Strictly speaking, the statement "the SJF scheduling algorithm has the minimum average waiting time and average turnaround time" is incorrect and imprecise.
The previous example shows that the Shortest Remaining Time Next algorithm yields even lower average waiting time and average turnaround time;
a condition should be added: "when **all processes can run at the same time**, the SJF scheduling algorithm has the minimum average waiting time and average turnaround time";
or "when **all processes arrive at almost the same time**, the SJF scheduling algorithm has the minimum average waiting time and average turnaround time".

If the above preconditions are not added, then it should be said that "the **preemptive** Shortest Job/Process First scheduling algorithm has the minimum average waiting time and average turnaround time".

Although strictly speaking, the average waiting time and average turnaround time of SJF are not necessarily the minimum,
compared with other algorithms (such as FCFS), SJF can still achieve relatively low average waiting time and average turnaround time.

## HRRN Algorithm

### Thoughts on FCFS and SJF

The FCFS algorithm selects the job (process) with the longest waiting time to serve at each scheduling decision.
However, it does not consider the running time of the job, which leads to the problem of being unfriendly to short jobs;

The SJF algorithm selects the job with the shortest execution time to serve.
However, it does not consider the waiting time of each job at all, which leads to the problem of being unfriendly to long jobs and may even cause starvation;

The Highest Response Ratio Next algorithm considers both the waiting time of each job and the running time.

### Response Ratio

**Response ratio** = (Waiting time + Required service time) / Required service time

The response ratio takes into account both the waiting time and the running time of the job, and the response ratio is always ≥ 1.

### Characteristics of the HRRN Algorithm

|Algorithm name|Highest Response Ratio Next (HRRN)|
|--|--|
|Algorithm idea|Comprehensively consider the waiting time and required service time of jobs/processes|
|Algorithm rule|At each scheduling decision, first calculate the **response ratio** of each job/process, and select the job/process with the **highest response ratio** to serve|
|Used for job/process scheduling|It can be used for both job scheduling and process scheduling.|
|Preemptive or not|Non-preemptive algorithm. Therefore, scheduling and response ratio calculation are only needed when the currently running job/process voluntarily gives up the processor|
|Advantages and disadvantages|Comprehensively considers waiting time and running time (required service time)<br />when waiting times are the same, the one with shorter required service time is preferred (advantage of SJF)<br />when required service times are the same, the one with longer waiting time is preferred (advantage of FCFS)<br />for long jobs, as the waiting time grows longer, their response ratio also grows larger, thereby avoiding the problem of long job starvation |
|Causes starvation|No.|

The **Highest Response Ratio Next algorithm** is a **non-preemptive** scheduling algorithm. Scheduling is only needed when the currently running process **voluntarily gives up the CPU** (normal/abnormal completion, or voluntary blocking).
During scheduling, **calculate the response ratios of all ready processes and select the process with the highest response ratio** to run on the processor.
