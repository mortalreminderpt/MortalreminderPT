In the three-state model of processes, processes ② and ③ are triggered by the scheduler, and the scheduler decides:
- Who runs? — Scheduling algorithm
- How long to run? — Time slice size

What events trigger the **scheduler** (process scheduling timing):
- **Creating a new process**;
- **Process exit**;
- Running **process blocks**;
- **I/O interrupt** occurs (may wake up some blocked processes).

The difference between non-preemptive and preemptive:
- With a non-preemptive scheduling policy, the scheduler is only triggered when a **running process blocks or exits**;
- With a preemptive scheduling policy, the scheduler is triggered on every **clock interrupt or every k clock interrupts**.

## Idle Process

If there are no other ready processes in the ready queue, the scheduler will select the idle process (Idle). That is, in an actual system, the CPU will not be idle; even if no ready process exists, the idle process will run.

Characteristics of the idle process:
- Lowest priority
- Can be a 0-address instruction, occupying a complete instruction cycle (interrupts are routinely checked at the end of the instruction cycle)
- Low energy consumption
